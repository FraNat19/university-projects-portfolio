Sorting: measured time against fitted complexity

Mean time in milliseconds over random integer arrays. Generated by Algorithm_comparison.ipynb.
nMergeQuickHeapTimsort (built-in)

Least-squares fits to T(n) = a·n·log₂n + b: merge sort a = 0.000159 with R² = 0.876, quick sort a = 0.000359 with R² = 0.933. The imperfect fit is measurement noise on a shared Colab runtime, visible as the non-monotone merge-sort timings at n = 5000 and n = 10000. CPython's Timsort runs one to two orders of magnitude faster than any of the three because it executes in C rather than in the interpreter.

Mean time in milliseconds. Generated by Algorithm_comparison.ipynb.

BFS and DFS stay within noise of each other, as expected from identical O(V+E) traversal cost. Dijkstra carries the heap overhead, and minimum spanning tree is consistently the most expensive of the four.
